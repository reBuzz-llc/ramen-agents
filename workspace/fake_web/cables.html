The cables under the ocean

Almost all intercontinental internet traffic travels through fibre optic cables lying on the seabed, not through satellites. A modern cable is about as thick as a garden hose. Most of that thickness is steel armour and insulation; the glass fibres in the middle are thinner than a human hair.

The first working transatlantic cable was finished in 1858 and carried Morse code. It failed within a month after an operator applied too much voltage trying to speed it up. A durable cable followed in 1866, laid by the Great Eastern, at the time the largest ship ever built.

Light fades as it travels through glass, so repeaters spaced every 60 to 100 kilometres boost the signal. They are powered by a direct current sent down a copper conductor in the cable itself, with the ocean serving as the return path.
