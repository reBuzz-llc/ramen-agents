What is actually alive in sourdough

A sourdough starter is a stable partnership between wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria. The yeasts, often Saccharomyces cerevisiae or Kazachstania humilis, produce the carbon dioxide that makes the bread rise. The bacteria, usually species of Lactobacillus, produce lactic and acetic acid, which give the sour taste and also keep other microbes out.

The partnership works because of a division of food. Many sourdough yeasts cannot digest maltose, the main sugar in flour, while the bacteria can. The bacteria eat the maltose and leak glucose, which the yeasts then use. Neither does well alone.

A starter that has been kept going for years is not really old. Each feeding replaces most of it with fresh flour, so what persists is the community, not the cells, much like a city.
