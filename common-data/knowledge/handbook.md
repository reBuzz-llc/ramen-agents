# Handbook: the formats the cards point at

Search this file by topic. It holds the detail that does not belong on a card.

## Task brief format

A task on the board is something a person must do. Imperative title that makes sense a year from now. Details in
Markdown: `### Why` (the context, why now), `### What to do` (numbered steps a person can follow alone, with the
names, links and figures they need), `### Done when` (a `- [ ]` checklist). A realistic Dead Line, today or later.
Priority P2 to P4; P0 and P1 only for work truly this week. Assignee: a person on the team, or empty.

## Outreach task format

One task covers a batch of leads, one a day at most. Title: "Outreach: <first business> and N more". Under
`### What to do`, one line per lead: business, email, package, row link, the angle to open with. New leads the same
day are appended to that task with `update_task`.

## Package email format

The pitch inside a package toggle: a plain greeting; the offer in one line (name, price, timeline, who it is built
for); the deliverables as a numbered list of two to six items, from the package record only; one short paragraph
inviting the reader to talk to the company's people; "Regards," on its own line and the company's name on the next.
The title carries the price. No slogan, no figure the record lacks, no feature the record lacks.

## The lead rule in detail

A lead is a business whose own site the bot read. The email is published on the business's own site, on a page the
bot opened; an address on another domain counts only when it is printed on the business's own page; a personal
mailbox provider never counts. No email, no lead. One business once. Location, industry and size only as a page
states them; an empty cell is honest, an invented one is not forgiven. The pain point is what the bot saw on their
pages that one of our packages fixes; evidence is the address of the page that shows it.

## Rate desk

When The CEO asks what something costs: answer with the figure or range and the page it came from, from at least two
other companies' own pricing pages, or "could not find" and why. Our own site is never a source for market rates.

## What we have

The owner's page about the company (services, team, products, rates) is the truth about us and outranks any market
figure. What is not on it is a proposal. FILL-ME: paste or sync that page into the bots' files.
